### Corrected worker stack failure

Real service acceptance originally aborted while initializing a native worker: its default stack was too small. The worker now explicitly reserves 8 MiB once at startup. The regression child sets `RUST_MIN_STACK=2097152`, so an ambient large-stack environment cannot conceal a missing explicit reservation. The rebuilt service passed native ready/drain/SIGTERM/output checks at both CPU caps. This proves the exercised lifecycle case; it does not establish that 8 MiB is the minimum possible stack or a throughput optimization.

## What native parity establishes

The retained study compared 850 tensors comprising 163,005,555 elements: 546 tensor checks used exact comparison and 304 used declared numerical tolerances. Values matched exactly in 805 tensors; that wording is not a bitwise identity claim for every floating-point payload. The largest absolute discrepancy was about `1.72e-5` in German reading-order scores and remained within the combined absolute/relative tolerance. All 14 expected text outputs matched the pinned reference.

The math gates include real batch sizes 1, 8 and 64 and heterogeneous termination. The six-row termination fixture uses 360 row steps, of which 158 precede or include row completion. The remaining 202 row steps motivated the now-completed same-age compaction experiment. Its measured ratios above include copies and preparation; the two-CPU regressions demonstrate why fewer row steps alone do not predict wall-time savings. It remains separate from production fresh-row refill and page/Tika throughput.

## Frozen quality study and interpretation

The main corpus has 33 primary pages in 19 document clusters and eight separate challenges. Its primary appearance mix is 19 printed, 11 handwritten and three mixed pages. English accounts for two primary pages, one cluster and 51 of 87,515 NFC reference letters: below 10% by each denominator. Public legal, historical, handwriting and script-specific inputs have documented reference qualifications; unknown training overlap and implementation exposure are disclosed.

The [main provenance audit](../evaluation/audits/main-metadata-recovery-20261019/README.md) verifies 113 of the original 119 entries: 97 data originals and 16 exact metadata originals. Its six ancillary metadata exclusions are explicit; the original 119-entry lock and all scored inputs/references are unchanged.

The two-case Portuguese laboratory supplement remains separate and descriptive. It contains methods-section crops from two published chemistry articles, not laboratory reports, result sheets, lab forms or clinical documents. No licensable public set of laboratory report or result-sheet pages has been admitted, so no result in this ledger covers such documents. Its protocol uses zero bootstrap resamples. The [pre-inference acquisition amendment](../evaluation/laboratory-supplement/audit/metadata-recovery-0002.json) already resolved its operational metadata blocker: two exact HTML originals were recovered, and two unavailable metadata bodies were explicitly excluded. Current replacement HTML is audit evidence only. Original audit records and all frozen PDFs, crops and references remain unchanged; original protocols remain recoverable at their recorded identities; [actual corpus admission passed](../evaluation/audits/acquisition-20261018/receipt.json).

The execution/report work must preserve all six retained variants × two CPU limits × two cohorts under the [always-math amendment](../evaluation/audits/always-math-20261019/amendment.json) and the [Indra 1.1 amendment](../evaluation/audits/indra-1-1-20261020/amendment.json), which binds both Indra arms to model revision `bd02167f` (v1.1.0, indra-ocr 1.1.2) with math, LM beam search and dual-read routing, including failed or unexecuted cells. Strict NFC CER, secondary whitespace-collapsed CER, valid tokenization, failure coverage and paired document-cluster intervals are separate views. Three timing repetitions do not triple the statistical accuracy sample. Performance units refer to successful case responses per complete workload wall second; mixed full pages and oracle crops do not become an undifferentiated primary-page throughput claim.

### Measured result (release run 39810108196)

On the 33 primary pages, Rust Indra and Python Indra produce the same first-attempt text: strict CER 26.589% and whitespace-collapsed CER 21.114% at both CPU budgets, against 28.297% (best PSM6), 28.564% (fast PSM3), 28.657% (best PSM3) and 29.328% (best PSM11) strict, and 22.315–23.070% collapsed, for Tesseract. On the two laboratory crops Tesseract scores 0.76–0.80% strict (best PSM11 1.98%) and the Indra arms 2.08%; the supplement is descriptive, with zero resamples.

Performance over the complete 41-case workload with three timing repetitions: at 1 CPU the Rust arm answers 1.457 successful case responses per workload second (median complete-body latency 567 ms, p95 1,158 ms), Python Indra 1.319 (721 ms, 1,311 ms), Tesseract fast PSM3 0.459 (2,151 ms, 3,676 ms) and the three best-model settings 0.254–0.261 (3,385–3,772 ms, 7,305–7,716 ms). The two budgets ran on separate hosted runners and the index records that difference; the numbers do not establish CPU scaling.

Every container in a cell had the full budget as its CPU quota inside one shared parent cgroup, so OCR could use CPU that Tika left idle. The shipped Compose and Kubernetes profiles instead give OCR and Tika fixed partitions (0.75/0.25 CPU and 1.5/0.5 CPU) that cannot borrow from each other; that configuration was not measured, and these numbers do not describe it.

Startup to readiness, one start per cell timed from container create, was 5.4–5.6 s for the Rust arm and 6.4 s for the Python arm. The runner samples readiness with serial `docker exec` probes, OCR service first and then the gateway, and sleeps 1 s after each failed probe; successive probes started 1.05–1.51 s apart, so each value is an upper bound with about one probe interval of resolution, and the 0.8–1.0 s difference between the arms is not resolved. In both Indra arms the gateway probe passed last.
